Technical Workshop Logs: Structural & Metallurgical Repair

Post-job documentation of structural dismantling, acoustic damping, and corrosion excision. These logs detail the specific mechanical and chemical processes utilized to address complex unibody degradation.

Hyundai Verna: Skeletal Dismantling & NVH Reduction

Observation: Vehicle exhibited excessive cabin resonance and poor panel alignment following substandard previous bodywork. Superficial repair protocols were insufficient to correct the underlying acoustic deficits.

Process: Executed a full skeletal dismantling, stripping the unibody to bare metal to inspect inner sills and hinge pillars. To address cabin acoustics, a 3-layer Constrained Layer Damping (CLD) system was applied to the door skins. This consists of a butyl-based mass layer, a closed-cell foam decoupler, and an acoustic barrier. Exterior panels were subsequently prepped, primed, and refinished.

Mechanical Note: Applying damping mats without utilizing a hard roller to press them into sheet metal contours leaves microscopic air pockets. In high ambient temperatures, trapped air causes the butyl adhesive to fail and delaminate, resulting in buzzing panels and degraded acoustic performance.

Precision Welding & High-Tensile Steel Repair

Observation: Side impact damage compromised the structural integrity of a luxury vehicle's door shell. Standard plastic body filler is contraindicated for structural load paths on modern Ultra-High-Strength Steel (UHSS) unibodies.

Process: The damaged section was excised, and replacement high-tensile steel was integrated using pulsed MIG welding. This technique controls heat input, preventing warping and preserving the metallurgical properties of the surrounding steel. The surface was finished using a multi-stage imported European basecoat system and a high-solids 2K clear coat to match the specific OEM refractive index of the factory finish.

Mechanical Note: Excessive heat input during welding on UHSS alters its grain structure, making it brittle in the Heat Affected Zone (HAZ). Strict adherence to OEM welding parameters (wire feed speed, voltage, and gas mixture) is mandatory to preserve crash safety ratings.

MG Hector: Corrosion Excision & Drainage Correction

Observation: Severe galvanic corrosion around the panoramic sunroof aperture. Moisture had breached primary seals, pooling in the roof cavity and causing subsequent ECU failure. The primary cause was blocked drainage channels combined with improper vehicle covering.

Process: Excised the corroded roof section using cold-cutting methods to avoid heat distortion. Replacement panels were fabricated from galvanized steel to prevent future zinc-oxide formation. Joints were MIG-welded, treated with zinc-rich epoxy primer, and sealed with polyurethane seam sealer. The entire roof was resprayed with 2K polyurethane paint, and sunroof drainage channels were mechanically cleared.

Mechanical Note: Applying a vehicle cover while the surface is still damp traps moisture against the seams, accelerating galvanic corrosion where dissimilar metals meet near the sunroof tracks. Sunroof drains must be verified with compressed air annually; a blocked drain forces water into the cabin headliner.
